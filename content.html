The Problem

Doing nothing falls behind. Doing it badly is worse.

AI is great at producing drafts and terrible at producing finished work. It turns out you still need a writer who can tell the machine what to do and fix the things it creates.

The trouble is you don't have that person in-house, or the one you've got is already buried. And hiring a whole agency costs more than the work is worth to you. So you're stuck between two bad options: publish nothing and watch competitors pull ahead, or publish AI slop that makes you look worse than saying nothing at all.

Meanwhile your leadership wants the marketing team to show results by working faster with AI, but demand has outpaced what anyone can actually deliver, and quality suffers for it. What's worse, readers can tell. It's getting easier to tell every month.

The hard part was never generating the words. It was deciding which ones are any good.

Why Now

The "just publish everything" era is over.

For a while you could point AI at a keyword list, publish in bulk, and rank on sheer volume. That stopped working. Search has gotten much better at spotting content that nobody wrote with a real reader in mind, and bulk, generic output gets you buried now instead of found.

So the bar moved. Quality is the strategy now. The companies winning the content game are the ones publishing fewer, better things that people actually read, finish, and link to. That takes a writer. That's me.
