The Problem

You're accelerating without a map.

AI coding tools have given your engineers 3–5x the output. They're so easy to use that designers and PMs are starting to mess around with the codebase. But the organizational knowledge that makes all that output coherent hasn't kept pace: code review is bottlenecking, unread docs are piling up everywhere, and uncooked features slip through without validation.

That senior engineer who held the whole system in his head is now managing who-knows-how-many AI agents, and none of them know what he knows. Nobody wrote down the rules of the world you're building, and it's starting to show: all those agents everyone's deploying are burning through tokens searching for context that should have been at their fingertips. Those costs are real now, and there's nothing stopping them from going up.

To do the work right and keep your token bills down, those fancy agents of yours need a map of the world they're building in, and you don't have one. Let's fix that today.
The Engagement

Scout the terrain, find the gaps, build the framework.

What you need is a translation layer between your different groups of people and their machine assistants. Let's build a scaffold that lets you build and maintain knowledge in a way that's easily traversable by both humans and AI. Think of it as the map that helps everyone — person or agent — know what they're talking about when they're talking about something, so they can work better, faster, and more efficiently together. Let's say six weeks:

Week 1

Discovery

I interview 5–8 people across engineering, product, and platform — and I sit with them while they work. I'm mapping the gap between what your platform team knows (the infrastructure) and what your product team knows (the users and the story). I identify where processes are breaking, where Claude goes off the rails, and where institutional knowledge lives in one person's head.

Weeks 2–3

Audit & Architecture

I deliver a gap analysis: what knowledge exists, where it lives, what's missing. Then I draft the architecture for your documentation — product context, system rules, traversal maps, coding conventions — structured so both humans and AI agents can use it effectively.

Weeks 3–6

Prototype

I pick the area where the pain is worst and build a working prototype: revised human processes and AI-accessible documentation for that part of your system. This might include new review workflows, agent-readable context files, a restructured knowledge base, or templates that help your AI generate better documentation going forward — whatever the discovery phase says you need most. You get the prototype, the method behind it, and a repeatable framework your team can extend across the rest of the system. I come back in six months to check what's changed.

Our goal is to help your assistants load context faster, get those agents to orient themselves without reading everything every time, and overhaul your review process so that everyone can work together more coherently. Bottom line: less time wasted, lower token costs, and features your users actually want that ship reliably. The math isn't complicated — even a modest improvement in any one of these pays for the engagement several times over.
